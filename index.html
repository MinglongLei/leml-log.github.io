About Me

I am currently an associate professor at the Beijing University of Technology. I received my Ph.D. in computer science at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, advised by Professor Yong Shi. Before that, I participated in the Joint Master Programme at the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, advised by Professor Tingshao Zhu.

Open to collaboration. I welcome inquiries from prospective students who are curious, self-motivated, and willing to develop strong research and programming skills. When contacting me, please include your CV and a brief description of your interests and relevant experience. I am also open to collaborations with researchers and practitioners working on related problems; feel free to reach out with a concrete idea.

Research Overview

"Connecting everything and observing the dynamics of the world!"

Learning on Graphs

This research focuses on deep representation learning for graph data, spanning structures at different scales, from molecules to social networks. It covers the modeling, analysis, and prediction of graph structures, with downstream tasks including node classification, link prediction, graph clustering, and graph classification. Applications include brain disease diagnosis, molecular property prediction, and anomaly detection. At the data level, the research examines properties such as sparsity, locality, and heterogeneity, with an emphasis on learning and representing implicit and higher-order structures. At the model level, it investigates expressiveness, generalization, robustness, and security, including cross-domain generalization, adversarial attacks, privacy protection, and model compression.

Spatiotemporal Data Mining

This research aims to extract valuable patterns and knowledge from data with both spatial and temporal dimensions. It addresses tasks such as spatiotemporal pattern discovery, anomaly detection, and forecasting. A primary application is traffic forecasting in intelligent transportation systems: modeling complex dependencies in historical traffic conditions across road networks to predict future states and support route planning, traffic signal control, and ride-hailing dispatch. The research seeks to improve the accuracy, efficiency, stability, and interpretability of traffic forecasting models, with particular attention to lightweight models, spatiotemporal heterogeneity, cross-city forecasting, and traffic foundation models.
